POPIA and TSL's POPIA Compliance

The StartUp Legal (Pty) Ltd  |  Version 2.0  |  Effective 1 October 2026

1. Status

1.1This addendum forms part of the Platform Terms of Use between The StartUp Legal (Pty) Ltd, registration number 2024/259720/07 (TSL), and the Subscriber. It applies from the date the Subscriber accepts the Terms of Use.
1.2It is the written contract required by section 21(1) of the Protection of Personal Information Act 4 of 2013 (POPIA) between a responsible party and an operator, to the extent that TSL acts as operator.
1.3On any matter concerning personal information this addendum prevails over the Terms of Use.
1.4Terms defined in POPIA carry the meaning given in POPIA. Personal information, special personal information, responsible party, operator, data subject, processing, security compromise and Regulator are used accordingly.

2. The Three Data Sets and Who Is Responsible for Each

2.1Account Data. Information about the Subscriber and its users held to operate the Account, including names, contact details, billing information, login records and usage records. TSL is the responsible party. TSL processes it under its Privacy Notice.
2.2Submitted Content. Personal information the Subscriber enters into or uploads to the Platform, including information about its directors, employees, contractors, customers and counterparties. The Subscriber is the responsible party and TSL is the operator. Clauses 4 to 13 govern this data set.
2.3Counsel Matter Data. Information provided to TSL in the course of a Counsel Session or a scoped engagement. TSL is the responsible party in its own right, because it determines how that information is processed in order to give advice and to meet its own professional and record keeping obligations. It is held subject to clause 15 of the Terms of Use and to clause 6.5 of the Legal Disclaimer and Scope of Service Notice.
2.4Where the same item of information falls into more than one data set, TSL applies the higher standard.

3. Subscriber Obligations as Responsible Party

3.1The Subscriber warrants that it has a lawful basis under sections 8 to 12 of POPIA for the personal information it submits, and that it has given the notice required by section 18 to the data subjects concerned.
3.2The Subscriber must not submit personal information it has no right to submit.
3.3The Subscriber must not submit special personal information as contemplated in section 26 of POPIA, or the personal information of a child as contemplated in section 34, except where the Blueprint expressly asks for it. Where a Blueprint does ask for it, the Subscriber is responsible for establishing the ground for processing under sections 27 to 33 or section 35.
3.4Where processing requires prior authorisation from the Regulator under section 57 of POPIA, obtaining it is the Subscriber's responsibility.
3.5The Subscriber is responsible for the accuracy of what it submits. The Platform does not verify it.
3.6Instructions to TSL as operator are given through the ordinary use of the Platform. Any other instruction must be in writing to hello@thestartuplegal.co.za and takes effect once TSL confirms it can be given effect. TSL may charge for an instruction that requires work outside the ordinary operation of the Platform.

4. TSL Obligations as Operator

4.1TSL processes Submitted Content only with the knowledge or authorisation of the Subscriber, and only for the purpose of providing the Platform and the services, as required by section 21(1)(a) of POPIA.
4.2TSL treats Submitted Content as confidential and does not disclose it, as required by section 21(1)(b) of POPIA, unless required by law or in the course of the proper performance of its duties.
4.3Where TSL is required by law to process Submitted Content otherwise than on the Subscriber's instruction, TSL will inform the Subscriber before processing unless the law prohibits it from doing so.
4.4TSL will tell the Subscriber if, in its opinion, an instruction would breach POPIA.
4.5TSL ensures that its personnel who have access to Submitted Content are bound by written confidentiality obligations and are given access only where needed.
4.6TSL does not sell Submitted Content and does not use it for its own marketing.
4.7TSL does not use Submitted Content to train a machine learning model. Anonymised and aggregated usage statistics that cannot identify a data subject or a Subscriber are not Submitted Content for this purpose.

5. Security Safeguards

5.1TSL secures the integrity and confidentiality of Submitted Content by taking appropriate and reasonable technical and organisational measures, as required by section 19 of POPIA. The measures in place are listed in Annexure B.
5.2TSL identifies reasonably foreseeable internal and external risks, maintains safeguards against those risks, regularly verifies that the safeguards are implemented, and updates them in response to new risks or deficiencies.
5.3TSL has regard to generally accepted information security practices applicable to a business of its size and sector.
5.4TSL may change the measures in Annexure B provided the level of protection is not reduced.

6. Security Compromise

6.1Where there are reasonable grounds to believe that Submitted Content has been accessed or acquired by an unauthorised person, TSL will notify the Subscriber immediately, as required by section 21(2) of POPIA, and in any event within seventy two hours of becoming aware.
6.2The notification will state what is known about the nature of the compromise, the categories and approximate volume of personal information involved, the likely consequences, the measures taken or proposed, and a contact point.
6.3TSL will assist the Subscriber in meeting its own obligations under section 22 of POPIA to notify the Regulator and affected data subjects. The decision to notify, and the notification itself, remain the responsibility of the Subscriber as responsible party.
6.4TSL will not make a public statement identifying the Subscriber in connection with a security compromise without the Subscriber's prior written consent, unless required by law.
6.5TSL maintains a record of security compromises affecting the Platform and makes the relevant entries available to an affected Subscriber on request.

7. Data Subject Requests

7.1Where TSL receives a request from a data subject relating to Submitted Content, including a request for access under section 23 of POPIA or the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000, or a request for correction or deletion under section 24, or an objection under section 11(3), TSL will not respond to it on the merits and will refer the data subject to the Subscriber, and will inform the Subscriber within five business days.
7.2TSL will assist the Subscriber to respond, taking into account the nature of the processing and the information available to TSL, and will provide the assistance within ten business days of a written request.
7.3TSL may charge a reasonable fee where assistance under clause 7.2 requires work beyond retrieving information from the Platform.
7.4TSL maintains a PAIA manual under section 51 of the Promotion of Access to Information Act 2 of 2000, available on the TSL website.

8. Sub-Operators

8.1The Subscriber authorises TSL to appoint the sub-operators listed in Annexure C.
8.2TSL will give the Subscriber twenty business days notice before appointing a new sub-operator or replacing an existing one. The Subscriber may object on reasonable data protection grounds within that period. If the objection cannot be resolved, the Subscriber may terminate the affected services on notice and receive a pro rata refund of prepaid charges.
8.3TSL will put in place a written contract with each sub-operator imposing obligations no less protective than those in this addendum before that sub-operator processes Submitted Content.
8.4TSL remains liable to the Subscriber for the acts and omissions of its sub-operators in relation to Submitted Content as if they were its own.

9. Transborder Flows

9.1Some sub-operators, including members of the Platform development team, are located outside the Republic of South Africa. Annexure C records the country of each.
9.2TSL transfers Submitted Content outside South Africa only on a basis permitted by section 72 of POPIA, being a binding written agreement with the recipient that upholds principles for the lawful processing of personal information substantially similar to those in POPIA and that includes provisions substantially similar to section 72 in respect of onward transfer, or where the transfer is necessary for the performance of the agreement between the Subscriber and TSL.
9.3TSL will not permit Submitted Content to be accessed from outside South Africa until the agreement described in clause 9.2 is in place with the recipient.
9.4The Subscriber may request a description of the safeguards applying to a transfer.

10. Retention, Return and Deletion

10.1TSL retains Submitted Content for as long as the Account is open and the Subscriber may need access to its Generated Documents.
10.2On termination of the Account the Subscriber has thirty days to download Generated Documents, Evidence Packs and a copy of its Submitted Content in a commonly used electronic format.
10.3After that period TSL will delete or de-identify Submitted Content within a further sixty days, save for material TSL is required by law to retain and material held in routine backups, which is deleted on the ordinary backup cycle.
10.4TSL will confirm deletion in writing on request.
10.5This clause does not apply to Counsel Matter Data, which TSL retains as responsible party for the period required by its professional and record keeping obligations, and in any event for at least five years from the conclusion of the matter.

11. Audit and Assurance

11.1TSL will make available to the Subscriber the information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this addendum.
11.2A Subscriber may audit compliance once in any twelve month period, on twenty business days written notice, during business hours, without unreasonable disruption, at the Subscriber's cost, and subject to confidentiality undertakings. Access does not extend to the data of other Subscribers.
11.3TSL may satisfy an audit request by providing an independent assurance report covering the relevant controls.
11.4A further audit may be conducted within the same period following a security compromise affecting the Subscriber.

12. Information Officer and the Regulator

12.1TSL's Information Officer is the Principal Attorney, contactable at hello@thestartuplegal.co.za.
12.2The Information Officer is registered with the Information Regulator as required by section 55 of POPIA and the accompanying regulations.
12.3Nothing in this addendum limits a data subject's right to lodge a complaint with the Information Regulator at the contact details published by the Regulator.

13. Liability and Duration

13.1Clause 16 of the Terms of Use applies to liability under this addendum, save that the limits in clauses 16.4 and 16.5 do not apply to an administrative fine imposed on the Subscriber under section 109 of POPIA to the extent that the fine arises from TSL's breach of this addendum, its gross negligence or its wilful misconduct.
13.2This addendum continues for as long as TSL processes Submitted Content, and the obligations in clauses 4, 5, 6, 9, 10 and 12 survive termination of the Terms of Use for as long as any Submitted Content is held.
13.3This addendum is governed by the law of the Republic of South Africa.

Annexure A: Description of the Processing

1 Subject matter. Operation of the TSL legal technology platform, generation of documents from structured question sets, and administration of Counsel Sessions.

2 Duration. For as long as the Account is open, and thereafter as set out in clause 10.

3 Nature and purpose. Collection, recording, storage, retrieval, use, transmission and erasure of personal information for the purpose of assembling documents from Subscriber answers, maintaining an Evidence Pack, and scheduling and delivering Counsel Sessions.

4 Categories of data subject. Directors, shareholders, employees, contractors, customers, suppliers, counterparties and advisers of the Subscriber, and individuals named in a Generated Document.

5 Categories of personal information. Names, identity or passport numbers where a Blueprint requires them, contact details, addresses, employment details, remuneration where a Blueprint requires it, company and directorship details, and the content of answers given in a Run.

6 Special personal information. Not collected, unless a Blueprint expressly asks for it. Clause 3.3 applies.

7 Children's information. Not collected.

Annexure B: Security Measures

These are the measures TSL applies to Submitted Content under clause 5.

1 Transport layer security at version 1.2 or higher on all connections to the Platform, using a current certificate. Private key material is held only in the production environment, is never transmitted by email under any packaging, and is rotated at least every twelve months and immediately on any suspected compromise.

2 Encryption of Submitted Content at rest using AES-256 or an equivalent standard.

3 Role-based access control. Access to Submitted Content is limited to personnel whose role requires it and is removed within one business day of a change of role or departure.

4 Multi-factor authentication on all administrative accounts and on all accounts with access to the production environment.

5 Logging of access to Submitted Content and to the administration dashboard. Access logs are retained for twelve months.

6 Separation of production, staging and development environments. Only synthetic data is used in non-production environments.

7 Daily encrypted backups, retained for thirty five days, with a restore test carried out at least quarterly.

8 A documented secrets and key management procedure covering issuance, storage, rotation and revocation.

9 Written confidentiality undertakings from all personnel, contractors and sub-operators with access to Submitted Content.

10 A documented security compromise response procedure aligned to clause 6, including the seventy two hour notification commitment.

11 Vulnerability management and patching. Vulnerabilities rated critical are remediated within fourteen days of a patch becoming available, and those rated high within thirty days.

12 Annual review of these measures by the Information Officer, and review after any security compromise.

Annexure C: Sub-Operators

1 TSL uses sub-operators in the categories set out below. The current list of named sub-operators, with the country in which each processes Submitted Content, is published at www.thestartuplegal.co.za/sub-operators and is updated in accordance with clause 8.2.

2 Platform development, deployment and support. Development and maintenance of the Platform front end and supporting services. Personnel may be located outside South Africa, in which case clause 9 applies.

3 Hosting and infrastructure. Hosting of the Platform and storage of Submitted Content, Generated Documents and Evidence Packs.

4 Payment processing. Processing of card and electronic funds transfer payments. Card data is captured by the payment provider and is not passed to TSL.

5 Transactional email and notifications. Delivery of account, booking and system messages.

6 Document assembly and storage. Assembly of Generated Documents and generation of Evidence Packs.

7 A provider is added to the published list only once the written contract required by clause 8.3 is in place, and, where the provider processes Submitted Content outside South Africa, once the agreement required by clause 9.2 is in place.
